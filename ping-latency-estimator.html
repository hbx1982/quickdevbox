Physics of Optical Fiber Latency and TCP Throughput

Network latency is bounded by the speed of light in silica glass optical fibers (approximately 200,000 km/s, or 5 microseconds per kilometer). Physical distance establishes an absolute lower bound on achievable Round-Trip Time (RTT).

1. Understanding Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP)

The Bandwidth-Delay Product measures the volume of data that can exist 'in flight' on a network link at any given instant:

BDP (bits) = Total Link Capacity (bits/sec) × Round-Trip Time (sec)

2. Impact of TCP Window Scaling (RFC 7323)

Legacy TCP implementations capped the receive window size at 64 KB (65,535 bytes). Over long-distance, high-bandwidth WAN connections (e.g. 1 Gbps across 100ms RTT), an unscaled 64 KB window limits maximum throughput to a fraction of available capacity. RFC 7323 Window Scaling allows TCP receive windows to scale up to 1 Gigabyte.
