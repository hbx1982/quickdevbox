Core Algorithmic Mechanics & Specifications

RSA (Rivest–Shamir–Adleman) is a public-key cryptosystem based on the practical difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers. Private keys are encoded using the PKCS#8 standard, while public keys use SubjectPublicKeyInfo (SPKI). The security of RSA relies on choosing sufficiently large primes (>=2048 bits) to withstand GNFS (General Number Field Sieve) factorization attacks.

Security Considerations & Best Practices

When implementing cryptographic algorithms in production systems, always ensure proper initialization vector (IV) uniqueness, adequate key entropy, and safe salt generation. Avoid weak legacy ciphers in high-risk environments and always prefer authenticated encryption modes like GCM or CCM where applicable.
